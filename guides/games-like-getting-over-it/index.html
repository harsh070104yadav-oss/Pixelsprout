Getting Over It on Pixelsprout (browser / Scratch remake)

Play it here: Getting Over It.

On Pixelsprout, Getting Over It is a fan-made Scratch remake by griffpatch, not the original game by Bennett Foddy. You guide a determined cat in a flower pot and climb a pile of giant Scratch code blocks above the water with a hammer. It is free, needs no download, and loads in the browser. The start screen credits it as a Scratch edition with Griffpatch.

How to start: wait for the embed to load, then choose New Game. A quick click may not start it — hold the click until the timer appears. The whole run is mouse-driven: the hammer head follows the cursor. Arrow keys and WASD do nothing in this version. There is no on-screen Mobile Controls toggle; touch play has not been tested on this page, so treat it as a desktop / mouse game.

Tips for Getting Over It–style games

These habits transfer between hammer climbs, hook jumps, and other precision physics games:

  1. Start the run cleanly. On our Getting Over It page, hold New Game until the timer shows. Watching the menu does not cost run time.
  2. Plant before you pull. Hook the hammer (or grab point) on a solid edge first, then pull. The pot only moves when the hammer head presses against something.
  3. Prefer slow, wide sweeps. Small, steady mouse movements give more control; fast flicks tip the pot.
  4. Settle between moves. Ease off when you land on a flat spot and wait a beat — leftover momentum can undo the last gain.
  5. Treat each block as a mini puzzle. Hook, pull a short distance, settle, then plan the next edge. Chasing a tall climb in one swing is how long runs end in the water.

Climbing & precision games like Getting Over It

Stickman That One Level — Guide your stickman through mind-bending puzzles and spikes, uncover paths to a golden key, and unlock the door. Same “one careful route” energy as a climb, with sharper reflex checks.

Carrot Climber — A rabbit climbs a wall by jumping from hook to hook for carrots. Aim carefully; miss and you fall. Lighter art, same precision demand.

Books Tower — Drop each sliding book onto a growing stack. Timing decides whether the tower grows or collapses — short rounds, high tension.

Buba Jump — Timed taps keep you alive as platforms dwindle. One-button control, steep skill curve, and a long drop when you mistime.

Robot Escape — Guide a robot through a vibrant pixelated escape. Precision and quick reflexes on a course where every move counts.

Canjump — One-button jumps across tougher terrain. Reflexes and timing matter more than story or combat.

Going Up — Tap-to-fly a bird through obstacles. Nostalgic arcade pacing with the same “one bad tap ends the run” feel.

Physics chaos that scratches a similar itch

Not every “like Getting Over It” search wants a pure climb. Some players want physics that fight back, fails that are funny, and another try one second later.

Falling Dummy — Drop a dummy from height and steer the fall for chaotic impacts. Creative chaos over careful climbing, but still physics-first.

Faily Skater — 3D endless skating through a chaotic city: dodge, trick, and recover from near-misses. Momentum and path choice matter.

Bottle Flip Game — Flip a bottle onto shelves and furniture. Tap timing decides a clean land or a tip — precision without a hammer.

Body Drop 3D — Ragdoll impacts with limited shots. Angle and gravity are the puzzle.

Car Crash Test — Real-time car physics and wrecks. Less climbing, more “watch the simulation punish a bad line.”
